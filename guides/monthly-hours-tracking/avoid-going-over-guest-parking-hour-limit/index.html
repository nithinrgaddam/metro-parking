How do I avoid going over my guest parking hour limit?

Updated 2026-11-11

Find out how your community counts guest hours: per vehicle, per unit or per visit, and over what period. Then keep a running total of hours used so far this month. Before you register a new visit, subtract that total from the cap and choose a duration that covers the visit with a little room to spare. Checking before you register matters more than checking after.

Find out exactly how your community counts hours

A cap on guest hours only helps you plan if you know how it is measured. Two communities can both say a set number of hours a month and mean very different things.

Ask your property management or leasing office, or check your resident guide, for these details:

  • The number and the period. How many hours, and whether the month is a calendar month or a rolling window.
  • What the cap applies to. One vehicle, one unit, or every guest at the property.
  • What counts as an hour. Whether a permit counts for its full length or only the time the car is parked, and whether overnight hours count like daytime ones.
  • What happens at the limit. A warning, a fee, a towing risk, or a block on new permits.

Write the answers down somewhere you will see them. Everything else in this guide depends on them.

Plan visits against month-to-date hours

Most people go over a cap by accident, not on purpose. One long weekend stay uses far more hours than it feels like, and the month is nowhere near over.

The fix is to look at your running total before you register, not after. Here is a simple way to do it:

  • Check the hours each guest vehicle has already used this month.
  • Subtract that from your community's limit to see what is left.
  • Look at the visits coming up: a friend staying overnight, a contractor all day, a relative who comes every Sunday.
  • Choose durations that cover the visit and no more. Booking a full day for a two-hour dinner burns hours you may want later.
  • Keep a buffer for surprises, such as a guest who stays longer than planned.

If hours get tight late in the month, think about which visits can move to next month, or whether a guest can park elsewhere for a day.

Counting by hand

A notes app or spreadsheet works. Log the date, the plate, and the start and end time each time you register, then add them up. It is reliable, but it is easy to forget an entry, and your total is only as good as your last note.

What it shows, and what it does not do

Be clear about one thing: the app shows you hours. It does not block a registration that would take you over your community's limit. You compare the number to your cap and decide.

A routine that keeps you under the cap

  • At the start of the month, note your limit and any long visits you already know about.
  • Before each registration, check the vehicle's month-to-date hours.
  • Register the shortest duration that fits the visit.
  • If you have Pro, turn on a reminder or calendar alarm so a guest does not overstay by accident.
  • Around mid-month, check again and adjust your plans for the rest of it.

The routine takes a minute or two per visit. It is much cheaper than finding out at the end of the month that you are over.

When tracking alone is not enough

Seeing your hours cannot change how your community counts them. If the limit applies per unit and you use several plates, add the per-vehicle totals together yourself.

If the numbers ever seem to disagree with what your property tells you, ask the leasing office how they count. For questions about the app itself, use the support page.

Frequently asked questions

Will Metro Parking stop me from registering a guest if I am over the limit?

No. It shows month-to-date hours for each vehicle, but it does not block registrations. You look at the hours, compare them with your community's limit, and decide.

How do I find out my community's guest parking hour limit?

Ask your property management or leasing office, or check your resident guide. Ask at the same time whether the cap is per vehicle or per unit, and whether it runs on a calendar month.

What if my community counts hours per unit instead of per vehicle?

Metro Parking shows hours for each vehicle, so you add up the vehicles that count toward your unit. Confirm with your leasing office exactly how they total them.

Can I get a reminder before a guest permit runs out?

Yes, with Metro Parking Pro. It notifies you before a permit expires and can add a calendar alarm at the exact expiration time. These are reminders, not a guarantee that a guest will leave.

Can I download Metro Parking today?

Not yet. It is an iPhone app that is coming to the App Store. Until then, a simple notes or spreadsheet log of dates, plates and times will do the job.
