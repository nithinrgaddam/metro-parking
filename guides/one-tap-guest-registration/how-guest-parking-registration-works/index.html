How does guest parking registration work in apartments?

Updated 2026-11-15

Most apartment communities that control visitor parking ask residents to register each guest vehicle. You get a parking passcode from your property management or leasing office, then enter your guest's license plate, a start time and how long they will stay. The permit is recorded in the property's guest parking system, and whoever patrols the lot checks plates against it.

Why apartments ask you to register guests

Visitor spaces are limited, and every community wants to keep them for actual visitors. Registration is how the property tells a guest's car apart from a resident's car, a neighbor's overflow vehicle or someone who simply wandered into the lot.

A registration is a small record: the vehicle's plate, the address or unit it is visiting, when the stay starts and when it ends. The property's guest parking system holds that record, and the people who enforce the lot use it to decide who gets a warning, a ticket or a tow.

That is why an unregistered guest can end up with a penalty even if they parked in a visitor space and only stayed an hour.

The resident side, step by step

The exact screens differ from one community to the next, but the flow is almost always the same.

  • Get the passcode. Ask your property management or leasing office for the guest parking passcode for your community.
  • Collect the guest's plate. Ask your visitor for their license plate before they arrive. Some systems also want the state, make or color.
  • Open the registration system. Enter the passcode and your unit or address so the permit is tied to your home.
  • Choose a start time and a duration. Pick when the permit begins and how long the guest needs.
  • Submit and keep proof. Note the end time so your guest knows when they need to leave or when you need to renew.

If a step asks for something your office has never mentioned, such as a unit code or a contact number, ask the leasing office rather than guessing. A wrong entry can leave a valid guest without a valid permit.

Where the passcode comes from

The passcode belongs to the property, not to you and not to any app. Only your property management or leasing office can give it to you, and some send it in a welcome packet, a resident portal or an email that is easy to lose.

If you cannot find yours, start with where to find your guest parking passcode. Once you have it, registering a guest vehicle with a parking passcode covers the next step.

Limits and habits worth knowing

  • Register before the guest parks. It is far easier than disputing a ticket afterward.
  • Watch the community's limits. Many communities cap how many hours a vehicle or a unit can use guest parking in a month. Check your lease or ask the office.
  • Expect permits to end. A permit runs for the duration you chose, so a guest who stays longer needs a new registration or has to move the car.
  • Cancel when plans change. If a guest leaves early, ending the permit keeps your hours from being used up.
  • Keep plates handy. Frequent visitors, such as family or a regular babysitter, are quicker to register when you do not have to ask for their plate each time.

The weak spot of the manual process is repetition. Retyping the same plate and the same details for every visit is where most mistakes and missed registrations happen.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to register my guest every time they visit?

Usually yes. A permit covers a set stretch of time, so a return visit needs its own registration. Saving the guest's plate makes repeat visits much faster.

Who gives me the guest parking passcode?

Your property management or leasing office. It is not something you can create yourself, so ask them first. Our guide on finding your guest parking passcode has ideas on where to look.

What happens if my guest stays past the permit?

That depends on your community's rules, but an expired permit can mean a warning, a ticket or a tow. Setting a reminder or alarm for the end time is the simplest protection.

Can I cancel a guest permit if my guest leaves early?

In Metro Parking, yes: you can cancel a permit right from the list in the Usage tab. Other systems vary, so check how your community's system handles it.
